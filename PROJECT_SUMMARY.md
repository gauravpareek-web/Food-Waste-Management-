### *A Simple, Jargon-Free Guide to the Problem, the Real Facts, and the Solution*

## 📖 What is This Document?
This guide breaks down the complex research, government data, and food safety rules behind food waste in India into simple, everyday language. It explains what the numbers actually mean, what food can legally and safely be donated, and how our platform works to solve the problem.

## 1. 🔍 The Big Numbers Explained Simply

You often hear shocking headlines about food waste in India. Here is what the verified official data (from the **UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024** and Indian Parliamentary records) actually says:

| What is being measured? | Official Figure | What it actually means |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Household Food Waste in India** | **~78.2 Million Tonnes** per year | Around 7.82 crore tonnes thrown away by homes every year. |
| **Waste Per Person** | **55 kg** per person / year | Each individual household member averages about 55 kg of food-related waste a year. |
| **World Average benchmark** | **79 kg** per person / year | Globally, household waste is higher (79 kg), but India's total volume is huge due to population size. |
| **Is all of this fresh food?** | **No!** | This 78.2 million tonnes includes **inedible parts** like banana peels, eggshells, tea leaves, and bones—not 78 million tonnes of ready-to-eat meals. |

> **Key takeaway in simple words:**  
> When pitching or talking about food waste, don't say *"India throws away 78 million tonnes of edible meals."* Instead say:  
> *"UNEP estimates Indian households produce 78.2 million tonnes of total food waste (including peels and scraps). Our project targets the recoverable portion of safe, unserved cooked food from commercial and institutional kitchens."*

## 2. 🍽️ Which Food Can Be Donated? (Food Safety Rules)

Not all uneaten food can or should be given to people. India's food safety authority (**FSSAI**) has very strict and sensible rules about surplus food redistribution:

### ✅ 1. Safe, Unserved Surplus Food (YES - We Donate This)
- Extra food prepared in clean kitchens that **remained in storage pans and was NEVER served to customers**.
- Kept at safe temperatures (Hot above 65°C, or Chilled below 4°C).
- Packed in food-grade, clean, sealed containers.

### ❌ 2. Customer Plate Leftovers (STRICTLY NO)
- Food that was served on a customer's plate or table and left uneaten.
- **Why?** It can carry saliva, germs, and cross-contamination. Distributing plate leftovers is strictly against health guidelines.

### ❌ 3. Spoiled or Expired Food (STRICTLY NO)
- Food that smells sour, has changed color, or stayed out past its safe consumption window.
- This goes to composting or organic waste processing, never to humans.

### ❌ 4. Kitchen Prep Scraps (NO - Waste Stream)
- Vegetable skins, peels, seeds, and bones.
- Useful for biogas and compost, not for hunger relief.

## 3. 🏢 The Restaurant & Commercial Kitchen Gap

- **Why aren't there exact national restaurant waste numbers?**  
  Studies usually look at one college canteen or one hotel. You cannot take one wedding hall and assume all restaurants in India waste the exact same percentage.
- **What our platform does:**  
  Instead of guessing, our app **records real, measured weights** every time a restaurant donates or discards food. This creates the first accurate, transparent record of commercial food surplus.

### Rajasthan & The Jaipur Pilot:

- **Why start in Jaipur?**

## 5. 🔄 The 5-Step Redistribution Journey

Instead of building a complicated social network, our platform executes one reliable, 5-step loop:

```
[1. Donor Posts] ➔ [2. NGO Claims] ➔ [3. Volunteer Collects] ➔ [4. Handover & Delivery] ➔ [5. Confirmed Received]
```

1. **Step 1: Restaurant/Donor Posts Extra Food**  
   The chef logs food name, quantity, preparation time, temperature, and safe expiry window.
2. **Step 2: Verified NGO Claims It**  
   Nearby registered charities get an instant alert and accept the food batch.
3. **Step 3: Volunteer Collects with Safety Checks**  
   A local volunteer is dispatched, checks packaging hygiene and temperature, and confirms pickup with a secure code.
4. **Step 4: Delivery to Shelter**  
   Volunteer transports the sealed food directly to the shelter or food distribution point.
5. **Step 5: Receipt Confirmation & Live Impact Update**  
   The NGO confirms the food was received safe and fresh. The dashboard updates meals rescued and CO₂ saved.

## 6. ⏱️ Why Time & Expiry Tracking Matters Most

Cooked food has a ticking clock. If a curry sits at room temperature for more than 4 to 6 hours, bacteria can multiply.

Our platform prevents food poisoning by tracking:
- **Cooked Timestamp:** Exactly when the kitchen made the dish.
- **Active Duration ("Kitna time chal rahi hai"):** How many hours and minutes have passed since listing.
- **Strict Expiry Cutoff:** The exact hour when the food can no longer be safely eaten.
- **Visual Color Bar:**
  - 🟢 **Green:** Fresh (<50% safe window elapsed).
  - 🟡 **Amber:** Moderate (Pickup must happen soon).
  - 🔴 **Red:** Urgent (<2 hours remaining).

## 7. 🎯 Honest Impact Reporting

Many apps make exaggerated claims like *"We saved 10,000 people from hunger"* when they only moved a few boxes of bread. Our platform follows honest, transparent rules:

- **Weighed Quantity:** Track actual kilograms offered vs. kilograms delivered.
- **Disclose Meal Size:** Clearly show the assumed weight (e.g. 350 grams = 1 meal).
- **Keep Composting Separate:** Composting is good for soil, but it is **not** a meal given to a hungry person. Only food distributed to and eaten by people counts as meals rescued.

## 8. 🛡️ Privacy & Sustainability

- **Privacy:** Public visitors only see approximate areas (e.g. "C-Scheme, Jaipur"). Exact kitchen addresses and contact numbers are only shared with the assigned volunteer and verified recipient NGO.
- **Long-Term Goal:** The best platform doesn't just pass surplus around forever—it helps kitchens notice which dishes are overcooked so they can **reduce excess food before it is even made**.
